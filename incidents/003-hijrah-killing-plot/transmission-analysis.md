## Independence findings

### Secure non-narrative anchor
Q 8:30 is not dependent on the later sīrah accounts for the proposition that a killing plot was remembered.

### Do not double count
- Ibn Hishām and al-Ṭabarī's quotation of Ibn Isḥāq are two surviving witnesses to substantially the same upstream Ibn Isḥāq material.
- Aḥmad 3251, al-Ṭabarānī 12155 and related versions through ʿAbd al-Razzāq → Maʿmar → ʿUthmān → Miqsam are one upstream family.
- Later tafsīr works that simply quote al-Ṭabarī or Ibn Isḥāq are not new corroboration.

### Potentially distinct value
- Qatādah material through Maʿmar is a different upstream attribution from Miqsam/Ibn ʿAbbās.
- ʿUrwa-related Hijrah material is another early trajectory.
- Early lexical tafsīr glosses have value for the meaning/context of Q 8:30 but should not be counted as witnesses to later scene detail.

## Source-critical tension

The detailed Miqsam route has a weak isnād in Arnaʾūṭ's assessment because of ʿUthmān al-Jazarī.

This does **not** weaken Q 8:30's killing-plot core.
It does weaken confidence in details that rely substantially on that route, especially:
- the precise overnight watch scene;
- pursuit sequencing;
- spider-web detail.

## Modern reconstruction check

Görke and Schoeler's reconstruction of the ʿUrwa Hijrah corpus finds that several broad Hijrah elements recur through multiple recensions and are plausibly early. Their reconstruction supports the broad migration framework more strongly than the fully dramatized Dār al-Nadwah scene.

Project rule:
modern source criticism informs the dependency analysis but does not replace source-level grading.
