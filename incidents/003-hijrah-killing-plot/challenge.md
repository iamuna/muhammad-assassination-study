# INC-003 — Adversarial Challenge Pass

## Result
**SPLIT — H1 conspiracy core retained; execution-stage details downgraded relative to it.**

## Challenge 1 — Could Q 8:30 be generic rather than a real historical plot?
The verse speaks retrospectively and concretely of opponents plotting to restrain, kill, or expel Muhammad.

Result:
The existence of a remembered hostile plot with killing as an option survives at very high confidence.

## Challenge 2 — Does the verse prove the Dār al-Nadwah story?
No.

Result:
Dār al-Nadwah, named speakers, detailed debate, and tactical rationale remain separately graded.

## Challenge 3 — Are Ibn Hishām and al-Ṭabarī independent witnesses to Ibn Isḥāq?
No.

Result:
They are treated as multiple surviving witnesses to an upstream Ibn Isḥāq stream, not two independent attestations.

## Challenge 4 — Is the detailed Ibn ʿAbbās/Miqsam route strong?
Not by itself.

Shuʿayb al-Arnaʾūṭ grades the isnād weak due to ʿUthmān al-Jazarī.

Result:
No high-confidence claim rests solely on that route.

## Challenge 5 — Is there narrative material outside the weak Miqsam route?
Yes.

Qatādah material and ʿUrwa-related Hijrah traditions preserve overlapping elements, although dependency and exact provenance must still be mapped.

Result:
Some execution-stage details remain plausible/probable rather than collapsing to H6–H8.

## Challenge 6 — Does “Quraysh plotted to kill him” equal an attempted assassination?
Not under the project's operational definition.

A conspiracy becomes an attempt only when execution begins through an overt step such as deployment, positioning, attack, poisoning, or approach.

Result:
- conspiracy: H1;
- attempted execution via house watch/positioning: H3 provisional.

## Challenge 7 — Can the spider-web story support the attempt classification?
No.

It belongs to a weak route and occurs after the departure anyway.

Result:
Spider web is irrelevant to establishing assassination intent or execution stage.

## Challenge 8 — Could the full scene be a literary expansion of Q 8:30?
Partly possible.

The verse supplies an obvious three-option skeleton that later narrators could expand. But early multiple narrative trajectories suggest that some pre-Hijrah plot framework circulated beyond a single late literary retelling.

Result:
Do not reject the entire narrative, but grade every expansion independently.

## Challenge 9 — Does ʿUrwa settle every detail?
No.

Modern reconstructions argue for an early Hijrah core but distinguish recoverable common elements from details that may belong to later transmitters.

Result:
Use ʿUrwa as an early framework witness, not a universal authenticity stamp.

## What would change the grades?

Upgrade execution-stage claims if:
- an additional early, demonstrably independent and strong route explicitly preserves the house watch/armed positioning;
- exact ʿUrwa recensions show that feature across independent transmitters.

Downgrade them if:
- Qatādah and ʿUrwa forms prove dependent on a later common narrative source;
- the house-watch detail can be shown to arise only in weak/later branches.
