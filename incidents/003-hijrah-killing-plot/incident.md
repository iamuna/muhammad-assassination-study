# INC-003 — Quraysh killing plot before the Hijrah

## Status
**Provisional source audit completed — 2026-09-18.**
Primary-source map and first adversarial pass completed. Exact edition/page locking remains incomplete for some secondary witnesses.

## Research question
What is the strongest historically supportable reconstruction of the Quraysh plot immediately before the Hijrah, and at what evidentiary level does it move from conspiracy to attempted assassination?

## Provisional conclusion

The evidence separates into two levels.

### Level 1 — Assassination conspiracy
**Very strong.**

Qurʾān 8:30 explicitly remembers a hostile plot against Muhammad with three alternatives:
- restrain/detain him;
- kill him;
- expel him.

Early tafsīr reports repeatedly interpret the verse in the Meccan context and preserve the same three-option core.

**Classification at this level:** assassination conspiracy.

### Level 2 — Overt attempted assassination
**Probable, but less secure than the conspiracy itself.**

Several early narrative streams add that:
- the plot was tied to the immediate pre-Hijrah night;
- ʿAlī occupied Muhammad's sleeping place;
- hostile watchers believed Muhammad was still inside;
- Muhammad escaped and was pursued.

These details are preserved in more than one trajectory, including an Ibn ʿAbbās/Miqsam route, Qatādah material, and an early ʿUrwa-related Hijrah complex. However, individual routes contain weaknesses and later expansions.

**Classification at this level:** attempted assassination, provisionally H3.

The book must therefore never write “the whole famous Dār al-Nadwah story is H1.” The H1-level proposition is the existence of a Quraysh plot in which killing Muhammad was explicitly one contemplated outcome.

## Evidence layers

### A — Qurʾānic core
Q 8:30:
`وَإِذْ يَمْكُرُ بِكَ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا لِيُثْبِتُوكَ أَوْ يَقْتُلُوكَ أَوْ يُخْرِجُوكَ`

Historical minimum:
- a remembered hostile plot by unbelieving opponents;
- directed personally at Muhammad;
- involving detention/restraint, killing, and expulsion as alternatives.

The verse itself does not name Dār al-Nadwah, Abū Jahl, a Najdī elder, the number of attackers, ʿAlī, or the cave.

### B — Early lexical/tafsīr layer
al-Ṭabarī preserves multiple early interpretations:
- Ibn ʿAbbās: `ليوثقوك` — to bind/restrain;
- Mujāhid: likewise restraint;
- Qatādah: to bind firmly;
- al-Suddī: imprisonment/restraint;
- ʿAṭāʾ/Ibn Zayd: imprisonment.

These reports independently reinforce the three-option reading but do not by themselves authenticate every later scene.

### C — Ibn Isḥāq / Ibn Hishām Dār al-Nadwah narrative
This stream gives:
- a formal council at Dār al-Nadwah;
- an elder from Najd;
- proposals to imprison, expel, or kill Muhammad;
- Abū Jahl's multi-clan killing proposal;
- the rationale of distributing blood liability across Quraysh;
- divine/Gabriel warning and permission to depart.

This is the principal source of the famous fully staged council scene.

### D — Miqsam / Ibn ʿAbbās route
Preserved through ʿAbd al-Razzāq → Maʿmar → ʿUthmān al-Jazarī → Miqsam → Ibn ʿAbbās, and transmitted in Aḥmad 3251 and elsewhere.

It preserves:
- the three options;
- ʿAlī sleeping in Muhammad's place;
- hostile watchers believing he was Muhammad;
- pursuit toward the cave;
- the spider-web detail.

Critical issue:
Shuʿayb al-Arnaʾūṭ grades this isnād weak because of ʿUthmān al-Jazarī. Earlier scholars differed, with some treating it more favorably.

The route therefore cannot carry the detailed scene by itself.

### E — Qatādah / Maʿmar material
ʿAbd al-Razzāq preserves a Qatādah version of the Dār al-Nadwah council:
- expulsion;
- confinement;
- killing through representatives of the tribes;
- the Najdī elder;
- departure with Abū Bakr;
- ʿAlī in the sleeping place;
- watchers mistaking him for Muhammad.

This is valuable because it is not simply the same Miqsam chain, though its relationship to other circulating maghāzī material still needs stemmatic testing.

### F — ʿUrwa Hijrah corpus
An early ʿUrwa-related Hijrah narrative, preserved in later sources, connects Quraysh's decision to detain/kill/expel Muhammad with:
- the immediate pre-Hijrah departure;
- ʿAlī in the sleeping place;
- search/pursuit;
- the cave.

Modern source-critical reconstruction by Görke and Schoeler argues that a substantial Hijrah core can be traced to ʿUrwa through multiple transmission paths. This supports an early Hijrah framework, but it does not automatically prove every detail in each surviving recension.

## Classification
- Direct assault: not established at H1 level
- Assassination conspiracy: **yes — H1 core**
- Attempted assassination: **yes, provisionally H3**
- Battlefield action: no
- General persecution: no
- Duplicate/misclassified: no
- Undetermined: no

## Adversarial result
**SPLIT.**

The Qurʾānic assassination-conspiracy core remains very strong.
The overt execution-stage details survive at a lower confidence level.
The famous dramatic council dialogue and cave-web material fall lower still.

## Narrative permission
The narrative may state directly that Quraysh plotted to kill Muhammad before his departure from Mecca.

The Dār al-Nadwah council, Abū Jahl's proposed mechanism, ʿAlī's role, and the house-watch should be narrated with precision reflecting their lower but meaningful support.

The Najdī elder/Iblīs, exact council speeches, and spider-web episode must be attributed rather than presented as unqualified historical fact.
