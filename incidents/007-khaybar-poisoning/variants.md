# INC-007 — Variants and Tensions

## 1. Who is said to confess?

### Anas family
A **Jewish woman** is brought and states that she intended to kill Muhammad.

### Abū Hurayrah / Saʿīd al-Maqburī family
A gathered group is questioned and admits poison was put into the sheep, giving an eliminate-him-if-false rationale.

### Historical implication
These reports agree on deliberate poisoning and hostile intent but differ in scene construction and the person/group answering.

Do not fuse the dialogue into one seamless scene without attribution.

---

## 2. Immediate pardon / refusal versus later execution

### Strong Anas report
Companions ask whether the woman should be killed; Muhammad says **no**.

### Jābir/al-Zuhrī and Ibn Isḥāq forms
He pardons/does not punish her.

### Abū Salamah / Abū Hurayrah-associated forms
Bishr dies; a later order is given and the woman is killed.

### Possible reconciliation
Later Muslim commentators often reconcile the material chronologically:
1. no execution for the attempt against Muhammad personally;
2. later retaliation after Bishr's death.

### Project position
This reconciliation is **plausible but must not be silently converted into fact** until the chains and chronology are fully audited.

Atomic claims:
- immediate refusal to execute — stronger;
- permanent pardon — weaker;
- later execution after Bishr's death — separately graded.

---

## 3. Did Muhammad swallow the poisoned meat?

Variants differ in presentation:
- canonical reports say he ate from it;
- Ibn Isḥāq's narrative says he chewed a morsel but did not swallow it;
- other maghāzī variants imply swallowing.

Therefore the narrative should not use over-precise digestive details unless the chosen sentence is tied to a specific source.

Safe core:
**He ate/tasted from the poisoned sheep and experienced effects attributed to it.**

---

## 4. Identity of the perpetrator

Canonical Anas material identifies:
- a Jewish woman.

Sīrah/maghāzī material identifies:
- **Zaynab bint al-Ḥārith**;
- wife of Sallām b. Mishkam in Ibn Isḥāq's form.

The personal name is not required to establish the assassination attempt and must be graded separately.

---

## 5. Motive

High-confidence core:
- eliminate/kill Muhammad if he were false/not a prophet.

Sīrah expansion:
- contextual grievance over what had happened to her people.

Popular later retellings sometimes sharpen this into revenge for a precise set of named relatives.

Project rule:
do not state a specific family-revenge psychology unless the exact source support is established.

---

## 6. Bishr b. al-Barāʾ

Several reports say Bishr ate from the sheep and died.

The detail is well represented outside the strongest Anas core, but the exact timing and mechanism need edition-level audit.

---

## 7. Final illness and cause of death

Strongly attested as a **reported statement**:
Muhammad is reported during his final illness as connecting ongoing pain to the Khaybar food.

Not established by historical texts alone:
a modern medical conclusion that a specific toxin from Khaybar directly caused his death years later.

The project must preserve the difference between:
- self-attributed lingering pain;
- traditional martyrdom interpretation;
- medical causation.
