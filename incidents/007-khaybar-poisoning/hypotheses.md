# INC-007 — Medical / Toxicological Hypotheses

These are **hypotheses**, not historical claims. They must never be promoted into narrative fact without evidence.

## HYP-007-001 — Intended lethal dose, insufficient ingestion for immediate death

### Proposition
The poison was intended to be lethal, but Muhammad ﷺ may have ingested too little of the toxic substance to die acutely.

### Historical support
- strong early evidence preserves explicit intent to kill/eliminate;
- Muhammad survived the immediate poisoning;
- reports say others ate from the same sheep, with Bishr b. al-Barāʾ reported to have died.

### Unknowns
- toxin;
- concentration;
- amount ingested by each person;
- absorption;
- vomiting/removal;
- individual susceptibility.

### Status
**Plausible mechanism; not demonstrated.**

### Historical confidence
H4 as a mechanistic reconstruction, not as an event claim.

### Narrative permission
NP-4 unless framed explicitly as a modern possibility.

---

## HYP-007-002 — Single acute sublethal exposure causing chronic injury

### Proposition
A single non-immediately-fatal toxic exposure at Khaybar may have produced persistent tissue or organ injury that caused symptoms for years.

### Historical support
- reports preserve later continuing pain attributed by Muhammad to the Khaybar meal;
- Abū Dāwūd also preserves an illness-period report connecting the final illness with the poisoned sheep.

### Toxicological plausibility
Modern toxicology shows that **some** acute poisonings can leave chronic or irreversible sequelae lasting years. This establishes general biological possibility, not identification of the Khaybar toxin.

### Critical limitation
No surviving source identifies:
- the poison;
- dose;
- pharmacokinetics;
- target organ;
- objective pathology;
- symptom chronology sufficient for retrospective diagnosis.

Therefore modern toxicology cannot establish that a particular toxin produced Muhammad's later illness.

### Status
**Biologically possible; historically and medically unproven.**

### Historical confidence
H5/open as a causal reconstruction.

### Narrative permission
NP-4.

## HYP-007-003 — Deliberately selected "slow poison" designed to kill years later

### Proposition
The perpetrator intentionally chose or dosed a poison so that Muhammad would deteriorate slowly and die years later rather than acutely.

### Evidence test
Current source audit has found:
- explicit intent to kill/eliminate;
- poisoning of a meal;
- no statement that the intended mechanism was slow death;
- no identification of a slow-acting substance;
- no evidence the perpetrator calibrated a sublethal dose.

### Status
**Unsupported by current evidence.**

### Historical confidence
H7 as stated.

### Narrative permission
NP-5.

---

## HYP-007-004 — Repeated/chronic poisoning after Khaybar

### Proposition
Muhammad was exposed to poison repeatedly over time after the Khaybar meal.

### Evidence test
Current sources describe a discrete Khaybar poisoning event. No evidence has yet been found for repeated dosing by the same perpetrator or others as part of this event.

### Status
**No supporting evidence currently identified.**

### Historical confidence
H8 unless new source evidence appears.

### Narrative permission
NP-5.

---

## Important terminology

"Slow exposure" can mean different things:

1. **Repeated low-dose exposure** over time — no current evidence.
2. **One acute dose with delayed effects** — biologically possible for some toxins.
3. **One acute dose causing permanent damage** whose consequences persist — biologically possible for some toxins.
4. **Deliberately engineered slow death** — currently unsupported.

These must remain separate.
