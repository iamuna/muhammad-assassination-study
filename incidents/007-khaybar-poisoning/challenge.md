# INC-007 — Adversarial Challenge Pass

## Result
**SPLIT — strong core retained; weaker details separated.**

## Challenge 1 — Is this only one story repeated by later collectors?
**No.**

After collapsing obvious duplicate publication, materially distinct transmission families remain, especially Anas, Abū Hurayrah/Saʿīd al-Maqburī, and Ibn ʿAbbās/ʿIkrimah.

Effect on grade: core retained at very high confidence.

## Challenge 2 — Is murderous intent merely inferred from poison?
**No.**

At least one strong Anas transmission preserves the woman's explicit statement that she intended to kill Muhammad. The Abū Hurayrah family independently preserves the logic of getting rid of him if false.

Effect on grade: intention remains core rather than inferred motive.

## Challenge 3 — Does the strong core identify Zaynab bint al-Ḥārith?
**No.**

The strongest canonical Anas wording identifies a Jewish woman but not her personal name. The name belongs chiefly to the sīrah/maghāzī layer.

Effect on grade: identity split from event.

## Challenge 4 — Do Bukhārī and Muslim count as independent corroboration for the Anas report?
**No.**

Their relevant versions share the Anas → Hishām b. Zayd → Shuʿbah trunk.

Effect on grade: independence score reduced; no change to the fact that the transmission itself is strong.

## Challenge 5 — Does Bukhārī's threefold appearance of the Abū Hurayrah report create three witnesses?
**No.**

3169, 4249 and 5777 are repetitions/abridgements of the same broad family.

Effect on grade: no artificial evidence inflation.

## Challenge 6 — Is the woman definitely pardoned permanently?
**Open.**

Strong material supports an immediate refusal to execute. Other reports say she was killed later after Bishr died.

Effect on grade: permanent-pardon claim not admitted as NP-1.

## Challenge 7 — Was she definitely executed?
**Probable but below core confidence.**

Execution appears in Abū Salamah/Abū Hurayrah-associated material and later source discussion, but chain relationships need exact audit.

Effect on grade: H3 provisional / NP-3.

## Challenge 8 — Did the poison directly cause Muhammad's death?
**Historically unresolved as a medical claim.**

The sources strongly preserve later pain and an attributed statement connecting that pain to Khaybar. They cannot provide toxicological proof across the multi-year interval.

Effect on grade: medical-causation claim H5/open; narrative may report the statement, not assert a medical verdict.

## Challenge 9 — Could this be ordinary wartime action rather than assassination?
**No under the project's operational definition.**

The reported method is covert poisoning presented as food after the fighting context, directed at Muhammad personally, with explicit lethal intent.

Effect on classification: attempted assassination retained.

## What would still force a downgrade?
- discovery that the supposedly distinct Anas / Abū Hurayrah / Ibn ʿAbbās streams collapse into a demonstrably single late source;
- serious textual evidence that the lethal-intent wording is secondary across all early forms;
- an early contradictory witness stronger than the converging reports.

No such result has been found in this initial pass.
