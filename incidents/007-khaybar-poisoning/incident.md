# INC-007 — Khaybar poisoning

## Status
**Provisional source audit completed — 2026-09-18.**
Exact-edition locking is underway; Aḥmad 2784 is now verified directly from SRC-HAD-AHM-E01 vol. 5, p. 6. Full narrator-by-narrator isnād audit remains pending.

## Research question
What is the strongest historically supportable reconstruction of the Khaybar poisoning, and does it meet the project's definition of an assassination attempt?

## Provisional conclusion

The **core event is exceptionally well supported by the standards of early Islamic history**:

- poisoned meat was presented to Muhammad ﷺ in the Khaybar context;
- he ate from it;
- the poisoning was deliberate;
- at least one early strong transmission explicitly states that the perpetrator intended to kill him;
- several transmission families preserve the same core independently enough that this is not a case of one late story multiplied by repetition.

**Classification:** Attempted assassination.

This conclusion does **not** automatically authenticate every later detail attached to the episode.

## Core transmission families presently identified

### A — Anas b. Mālik family
Anas → Hishām b. Zayd → Shuʿbah → collector branches.

Preserved in:
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 2617
- Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 2190a-b

Important dependency note: Bukhārī 2617 and Muslim 2190a are not two fully independent upstream witnesses; they share the Anas → Hishām → Shuʿbah trunk.

Core content:
- a Jewish woman brought poisoned meat/sheep;
- Muhammad ate;
- she was brought before him;
- Muslim's version explicitly preserves her statement: **"I intended to kill you"**;
- Anas reported continuing visible effects in Muhammad's mouth/throat area;
- companions asked whether she should be killed and Muhammad answered no at that point.

### B — Abū Hurayrah / Saʿīd al-Maqburī family
Abū Hurayrah → Saʿīd b. Abī Saʿīd al-Maqburī → al-Layth → Bukhārī branches.

Preserved in:
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 3169
- 4249 (abridged)
- 5777

Important dependency note: these three Bukhārī locations are repetitions/abridgements of the same broad transmission family, not three independent witnesses.

Core content:
- Khaybar setting;
- poisoned sheep;
- admission that poison was placed in the sheep;
- motive framed as: if Muhammad were false, they would be rid of him; if a prophet, poison would not harm him.

### C — Ibn ʿAbbās family
Ibn ʿAbbās → ʿIkrimah → Hilāl b. Khabbāb → ʿAbbād b. al-ʿAwwām.

Preserved in:
- Musnad Aḥmad **2784** in the verified project edition (SRC-HAD-AHM-E01, vol. 5, p. 6)
- Ibn Saʿd, al-Ṭabaqāt (parallel form)

Verified editorial judgment for Aḥmad 2784:
- report: **ṣaḥīḥ li-ghayrihi**
- this specific isnād: **ḥasan**

Core content:
- Jewish woman;
- poisoned sheep;
- interrogation about motive;
- explicit eliminate-him-if-not-prophet logic;
- later recurrent physical effects/cupping.

This is important because it is not simply the Anas or Saʿīd al-Maqburī line republished.

### D — Jābir / al-Zuhrī family
Jābir b. ʿAbd Allāh → al-Zuhrī → Yūnus → Ibn Wahb.

Preserved in:
- Sunan Abī Dāwūd 4510

Caution:
- this route has been criticized as disconnected because al-Zuhrī's direct hearing from Jābir is disputed/denied in hadith criticism;
- it should not be weighted like the strongest connected families without further audit.

### E — Abū Salamah / Muḥammad b. ʿAmr family
Variants occur as:
- mursal Abū Salamah material in Abū Dāwūd 4511/4512;
- connected Abū Salamah → Abū Hurayrah forms in later preserved routes such as al-Ṭabarānī/al-Ḥākim.

This family is central to:
- Bishr b. al-Barāʾ's death;
- the report that the woman was later killed.

Caution:
the exact relationship between the mursal and connected forms must be mapped before final grading.

### F — Sīrah/Maghāzī family
Ibn Isḥāq as preserved through Ibn Hishām and quoted by later historians; related al-Zuhrī/Mūsā b. ʿUqbah material also survives in later compilations.

This family supplies important details absent from the strongest canonical core:
- the name **Zaynab bint al-Ḥārith**;
- her relationship to Sallām b. Mishkam;
- asking which part of the sheep Muhammad preferred;
- concentrating poison in the foreleg;
- Bishr swallowing while Muhammad did not;
- explicit contextual motive language;
- pardon language.

These details are not discarded, but must be graded independently.

### G — ʿĀʾishah final-illness family
ʿĀʾishah → ʿUrwah → al-Zuhrī → Yūnus → ʿAnbasah → later collectors.

Bukhārī 4428 cites the report in suspended form; connected forms are preserved by al-Bazzār, al-Ḥākim, al-Bayhaqī and discussed by Ibn Ḥajar.

Core content:
- during his final illness Muhammad reportedly said he still felt the pain of the food eaten at Khaybar and used the image of his aorta being cut by the poison.

Historical consequence:
this strongly supports **reported lingering pain and Muhammad's own attributed connection of that pain to Khaybar**. It does **not by itself medically prove** that the poison was the direct biological cause of death years later.
