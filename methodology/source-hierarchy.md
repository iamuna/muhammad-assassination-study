# Source Hierarchy and Evaluation

This project does not use a simplistic rule that one genre is automatically reliable and another automatically unreliable.

## Evidence families

### Qur'an
Early textual evidence, often highly valuable for the existence of a conflict, accusation, plot, or social context. Usually allusive rather than biographically exhaustive.

### Hadith collections
Evaluated report by report. Canonical status is recorded but does not remove the need to examine chain, wording, variants, and what the report actually proves.

### Sīrah and Maghāzī
Essential historical evidence. Reports must be analyzed individually for provenance, transmission, dependence, chronology, and narrative expansion.

### Ṭabaqāt / biographical literature
Can preserve early material otherwise lost. Later compilation date does not automatically mean late origin; source dependencies must be reconstructed.

### Tafsīr
Useful when a verse is connected to an event, but exegetical traditions can preserve multiple competing historical identifications.

### Later chronicles and devotional works
May preserve earlier material but carry greater dependence/redaction risks. They are not discarded, but their evidentiary role must be explicit.

### Modern scholarship
Used for source criticism, manuscript/edition history, chronology, historical context, and competing reconstructions. Modern authority never substitutes for primary evidence.

## Independence rule

Multiple books are not counted as multiple confirmations until their transmission relationship is tested.

Example:

```
Earlier report
├── branch A → later collector 1
└── branch B → later collector 2

Ibn Ishaq stream
├── Ibn Hisham
└── al-Tabari quotation
```

This may represent two broad evidence streams, not four independent witnesses.

## Source versus event

A source is evidence **for** a historical proposition. It is not identical to the event itself.

Therefore every final historical statement must distinguish:
- explicit source content;
- inference from that content;
- modern reconstruction;
- unresolved uncertainty.
