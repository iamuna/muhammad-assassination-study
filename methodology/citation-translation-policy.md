# Citation, Translation, and Transliteration Policy

## Purpose

The publication must make it possible to move from narrative prose back to the exact source wording without ambiguity.

## 1. Citation system

Final publication style: **Chicago Notes and Bibliography**.

Repository research records use stable internal IDs in addition to conventional citations.

### Qurʾān
Primary locator: sūrah:āyah, e.g. `Q 8:30`.

Edition/page is normally unnecessary because verse numbering is stable for the passages used in this project. Any material textual/reading issue must receive a separate textual note.

### Hadith
Every citation should preserve:
1. collection;
2. book/chapter where useful;
3. standard hadith number;
4. locked edition ID;
5. volume/page in the locked edition when verified;
6. route/transmission family ID where relevant.

Do not rely on a website's display numbering as the sole publication locator.

### Sīrah / Maghāzī / Chronicles
Cite:
1. author/work;
2. edition ID;
3. volume/page;
4. transmission attribution in the text when historically material.

If al-Ṭabarī quotes Ibn Isḥāq, the evidence record must distinguish **al-Ṭabarī as the surviving witness** from **Ibn Isḥāq as the attributed upstream source**.

## 2. Arabic source text

Arabic is preserved when:
- exact wording affects interpretation;
- intention or motive is explicit in the wording;
- variants alter historical meaning;
- an exact quotation appears in the narrative;
- translation is contested.

The repository stores the Arabic exactly as printed in the locked edition, apart from clearly documented normalization of searchable Unicode.

Never silently harmonize variant Arabic texts.

## 3. Translation layers

Each important passage may have two translations.

### LIT — literal evidentiary translation
Purpose: analysis.
- follows the syntax and semantic range closely;
- avoids polishing away ambiguity;
- supplies bracketed words only when necessary;
- preserves uncertainty.

### NAR — readable narrative translation
Purpose: published prose.
- natural English;
- cannot add propositions absent from the Arabic;
- cannot strengthen intention, causation, chronology, or certainty;
- must map to the LIT translation.

If the NAR version materially departs from the LIT version, the difference must be documented.

## 4. Translation provenance

Default rule: **our own translation from the locked Arabic edition** for evidentiary passages.

Published translations may be consulted and cited, but language is not silently copied from them.

Where an existing translation materially affects interpretation, quote it only within copyright limits and cite it separately.

## 5. Transliteration

### Source book
Use scholarly transliteration with meaningful consonantal distinctions:
- ʿ for ʿayn
- ʾ for hamzah where useful
- macrons for long vowels: ā, ī, ū
- dotted consonants where standard: ḥ, ṣ, ḍ, ṭ, ẓ

Examples:
- Muḥammad
- ʿAlī
- Ṣafwān
- Banū al-Naḍīr
- Khaybar

### Narrative book
Use a lighter readability layer while preserving identity and avoiding confusion.

Default examples:
- Muhammad
- Ali
- Abu Bakr
- Quraysh
- Khaybar

At first mention or in notes, scholarly form can be supplied when useful.

### Exception
Canonical work titles in the bibliography retain scholarly transliteration.

## 6. Names and place registry

Every person/place will eventually receive a stable entity ID with:
- Arabic spelling;
- scholarly transliteration;
- narrative spelling;
- variant spellings/nisbahs;
- disambiguation notes.

This prevents one person from being accidentally represented as two because of transliteration differences.

## 7. Exact quotation rule

A line of dialogue is not admitted merely because the event itself is strong.

For every quotation:
- grade the existence of the event;
- grade that the speaker said something of this kind;
- grade the exact wording separately.

A strong event with unstable wording may be paraphrased but not quoted verbatim.

## 8. Religious formulas and honorifics

The repository preserves source wording without inserting honorifics into quotations that are absent from the cited text.

Editorial honorific style in the final narrative is a publication-style choice and does not affect historical grading.

## 9. Dates

Prefer:
- Hijrī year where it is the primary traditional chronological frame;
- CE equivalent when responsibly convertible;
- explicit uncertainty where month/day conversion is unstable.

Do not invent exact Gregorian dates merely for narrative smoothness.

## 10. Web sources

Websites are discovery and provisional verification tools unless they reproduce a locked edition reliably.

Before publication, major claims should point to:
- the locked edition;
- a stable academic/library record;
- or a primary-source scan.

A popular website repeating a source is not an independent witness.
