# Historical Evidence Grading System

## Principle

Grade **claims**, not whole stories.

An incident may contain an H1 core and H5 details. A strong core does not automatically authenticate later narrative expansion.

## Claim types

- E — event/existence
- A — action
- I — intention
- Q — quotation
- N — identity/name
- T — chronology
- L — location
- M — motive
- C — causal claim
- S — supernatural/theological claim
- D — descriptive detail

## Twelve scoring axes

Each axis is scored 0–10, where 10 means strongest historical support / lowest concern.

1. **SP — Source Proximity**: closeness of surviving evidence to the event.
2. **II — Isnād Integrity**: connectedness and critical strength of transmission.
3. **MI — Matn Integrity**: textual coherence and absence of serious anomaly.
4. **CO — Corroboration**: support from additional evidence.
5. **IN — Independence**: whether corroborating streams are genuinely independent.
6. **VS — Variant Stability**: stability of the core claim across transmissions.
7. **CF — Contextual Fit**: chronology, geography, politics, tribal relations, social setting.
8. **GR — Genre Reliability**: evidentiary risk arising from literary genre and purpose.
9. **AR — Anachronism Resistance**: absence of signs of later projection.
10. **PL — Plausibility**: physical and historical feasibility.
11. **RR — Redaction Resistance**: low risk that later editorial reshaping created the claim.
12. **EC — Explanatory Competition**: strength of the claim relative to plausible alternatives.

### Default weights

- SP 12%
- II 14%
- MI 10%
- CO 14%
- IN 10%
- VS 8%
- CF 8%
- GR 5%
- AR 6%
- PL 5%
- RR 5%
- EC 3%

The weighted score is **diagnostic, not dispositive**. Hard evidentiary rules can override a numerical result.

## Hard rules

- Twenty dependent retellings do not equal twenty witnesses.
- Plausibility cannot repair a broken chain by itself.
- A strong isnād does not automatically authenticate every interpretation attached to a report.
- Later narrative expansion is graded separately from the earliest core.
- Exact dialogue receives its own grade.
- A claim contradicted by materially stronger evidence cannot retain a high class merely through repetition.
- Missing evidence is not evidence of absence unless the silence is itself historically probative.

## Confidence classes

- **H1 — Virtually Secure**: exceptional evidence by the standards of early Islamic history.
- **H2 — Very Strong**: strong reason to accept the historical core.
- **H3 — Strong**: probably historical; meaningful uncertainty remains.
- **H4 — Moderate**: plausible and supported, but qualification is required.
- **H5 — Open**: evidence genuinely inconclusive.
- **H6 — Weak**: possible but poorly supported.
- **H7 — Very Weak**: serious evidentiary problems.
- **H8 — Historically Unusable**: cannot responsibly support a factual assertion.
- **R — Rejected/Misclassified**: contradicted, duplicated, demonstrably erroneous, or not actually an assassination attempt under the project definition.

## Modifiers

Examples:
- `-C` contested detail
- `-V` variant instability
- `-I?` intention uncertain
- `-N?` identity uncertain
- `-Q?` exact wording uncertain
- `-T?` chronology uncertain
- `-L?` location uncertain

## Narrative Permission

- **NP-1** — may be stated directly as historical fact.
- **NP-2** — may be stated directly but avoid unnecessary precision.
- **NP-3** — narrate only with attribution ("According to Ibn Ishaq..." etc.).
- **NP-4** — source-book/endnote discussion only.
- **NP-5** — do not place in narrative as history.

## Fiction Contamination Index

Every narrative sentence can be audited:

- **FCI-0** — sourced fact.
- **FCI-1** — neutral connective prose.
- **FCI-2** — reasonable physical/contextual inference.
- **FCI-3** — literary reconstruction; exceptional and must be visible as reconstruction.
- **FCI-4** — unsupported psychological inference; remove.
- **FCI-5** — invented historical content; prohibited.

Target: narrative prose should remain within FCI 0–2. FCI-3 is exceptional. FCI-4/5 are excluded.
