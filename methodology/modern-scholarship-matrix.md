# Modern Scholarship Method Matrix

Modern scholarship is used to **test methods and reconstructions**, not to replace primary evidence.

## Method families deliberately represented

### 1. Isnād-cum-matn / reconstructive
- Harald Motzki
- Andreas Görke
- Gregor Schoeler

Use for:
- transmission-family reconstruction;
- common-link and partial-common-link analysis;
- testing whether apparently late written compilations preserve earlier material;
- separating shared cores from later expansions.

Risk to watch:
- overconfidence in recoverability from structured transmission data.

### 2. Balanced critical synthesis
- Sean W. Anthony

Use for:
- combining Muslim literary sources with documentary and non-Muslim evidence;
- Late Antique context;
- avoiding both blanket acceptance and blanket rejection.

Risk to watch:
- broad synthetic judgments may still rely on disputed source chronologies.

### 3. Revisionary macro-history
- Fred M. Donner

Use for:
- challenging inherited assumptions about community identity and early chronology;
- wider seventh-century framing.

Risk to watch:
- macro-models should not be allowed to overrule incident-level evidence without direct support.

### 4. Literary/textual analysis
- Uri Rubin

Use for:
- detecting narrative shaping;
- comparing thematic development across sīrah reports;
- distinguishing literary function from historical core.

Risk to watch:
- literary explanation is not itself proof that an event is unhistorical.

### 5. Classical-hadith methodology in modern synthesis
- Jonathan A. C. Brown

Use for:
- understanding classical narrator criticism;
- terminology;
- institutional history of hadith authentication;
- avoiding anachronistic use of ṣaḥīḥ/ḥasan/ḍaʿīf labels.

Risk to watch:
- classical hadith authenticity and modern historical probability are related but not identical questions.

### 6. External-source control
- Robert G. Hoyland

Use for:
- Christian, Jewish, Zoroastrian, and documentary witnesses;
- seventh/eighth-century external chronology and context.

Risk to watch:
- silence in external sources is rarely decisive for small-scale incidents.

### 7. Strong skeptical challenge
- Patricia Crone
- Stephen J. Shoemaker

Use for:
- red-team testing of geography, chronology, source dependence, and later reconstruction;
- ensuring the project does not simply inherit traditional narrative structure.

Risk to watch:
- revisionary alternatives also require positive evidence and cannot receive a lower burden of proof merely because they are skeptical.

## Project rule

No modern scholar receives an authority score that can automatically decide a historical claim.

For each claim:
1. primary evidence is mapped first;
2. modern analyses are attached to the exact point they illuminate;
3. disagreements are recorded;
4. the project states which argument is stronger only at the **historical-evidence level**, not because one scholar is preferred globally.
